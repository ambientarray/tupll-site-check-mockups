Strong. This location scores higher than 85 percent of existing independent coffee shop locations in Texas.

The index is built from what is around the address: who lives nearby, who works nearby by day, what else is open on the surrounding blocks, how the streets and buildings are arranged, and what the local coffee market already looks like.

Read it as a comparison. 130 means the location looks about 30 percent more promising than a typical existing location. 80 means about 20 percent less.

It is a relative score, not a percentage or a grade. Each address gets one number, without a range, and the number says nothing about sales.

What it measures

Tupll compares locations, not businesses.

It answers one question: if the same owner opened the same shop in each of these places, which place gives better odds?

It cannot see the things that are up to you or specific to the site. A great operator in an average location can outperform a weak operator in a great one. Use the number to choose between locations and to spot problems early, alongside your own judgment and a visit to the site.

Not measured

  • The quality of your product
  • Your staff and service
  • Your prices and hours
  • The condition of the building
  • Your rent
  • Parking
  • Signage
  • How easy it is to pull in and out of the lot

The model

How we built
and tested it.

We started with every coffee shop in Texas we could identify, about 8,500 locations, 5,800 of them independents. For each one we measured the neighborhood at four distances, from a half mile out to ten miles, using public data: the census, federal employment records, state business permits, road and land data, and an open map of businesses. Then we asked one question. Knowing only what is true about the area around a shop, and nothing about the shop itself, how well can we predict how much custom it attracts?

To be sure the answer holds on ground the model has never seen, we trained it on some regions of Texas and tested it on whole regions it had never seen. We discarded every training shop within ten miles of a test region, so the model could not simply remember the shop down the road. And we kept one final set of locations sealed until every decision was fixed. We predicted a score of 0.403 on that sealed set and scored 0.393.

What the test showed

  • Locations this model puts in its top ten percent are about 1.9 times as likely to be genuinely top-tier locations as a location picked at random.
  • Pick a location blind and roughly one in five is bottom-tier. Among the locations the model ranks highest, it is about one in fourteen.
  • Comparing sites within one city, it is about two times better than chance.

What it does not mean

Those are statements about how often the model is right, not about how many customers a shop will get. No location is promised 1.9 times the trade of another.

Every location the model learned from is in Texas, which is why Site Check covers Texas addresses only.

We also tested whether location alone predicts if a shop is still trading three years later. It barely does, so we do not publish a survival score.

Before you order

Questions.

Who is this for?

An independent owner considering one or more Texas addresses for a coffee shop, including a first-time owner deciding whether to sign a lease. More business types will follow.

What will I receive, and when?

A web report for your address with the Location Index, the twelve neighborhood statistics behind it, and the neighborhood detail. It arrives within three business days of payment. Your delivery date appears before payment and is repeated in a confirmation email.

Can it tell me whether my shop will succeed?

No. Tupll compares locations, not businesses. The number is relative: it shows how this location compares with the locations of existing independent coffee shops in Texas. It says nothing about sales, and it cannot see your product, staff, rent, building, parking, or how easy it is to pull into the lot.

What does 100 mean?

Typical for the locations of existing independent coffee shops in Texas. It is not a percentage and not a grade. Above 100, the location compares better than typical on what Tupll measures; below 100, it compares worse.

Does the number come with a range or a confidence level?

No. Each address gets one number. Treat small differences between addresses as ties, and use the number first to rule out weak locations, which is where it is most reliable.

I am comparing several addresses. How does that work?

Order them together. One address is $139. Two to five in the same order are $119 each, and six to ten are $103 each. You see them side by side in one table, sorted by the index.

Where does the data come from?

Public records, census and mapping data: the census, federal employment records, state business permits, road and land data, and an open map of businesses. The report names its sources and the period each one describes.
